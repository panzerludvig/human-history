## 15 — A probe that checks the shader against its CPU source

Two commits: `7e704b8`, the probe (a hidden GL context reading the shader's values for 600 points per world through an off-screen target, compiled only under `MIRROR_PROBE`), and `f5029ab`, the shader brought back to the CPU's rules.

**What it found.** Two drifts the order assumed were gone after 06. The swamp: the CPU's rule changed (`atmosphere::swampFromBalance`) and the shader kept the old one, so the globe draws more marsh than the simulation has. The poles: the shader clamps its climate lookup at 86.4 degrees and the CPU does not, up to 1.9 C apart on Earth.

**Probe.** At `f5029ab` the probe passes on seed 7 and Earth, every quantity within a tolerance of four times its measured largest difference, every class disagreement at a boundary; changing the shader's lapse rate to 7.0 makes it fail on temperature, and restoring it passes. At `7e704b8` the five screenshots are identical to `main`, so the probe's readout changes nothing; at `f5029ab` four of them change, by the two fixes. Done when 4 asks for them identical, so the order failed. It also conflicts with 09 in `globe.frag`, `main.cpp` and both Technical notes in a way that needs lines neither wrote, so it would have been deferred.

**For the morning.** Whether the shader follows the CPU on the swamp and the poles: `standards/general.md` makes the CPU the source of truth and a drift a bug, which says keep `f5029ab`; the order held the picture fixed because it assumed the rules agreed. Either way the order goes back to planned, to be redone on `main` after 09, with Done when 4 saying what may change. Tolerances were measured on one GPU.

**Verdict:** rework. The CPU is the source of truth: the shader follows it on the swamp and the poles and on any further drift the probe finds, and the picture may change by those fixes. Back to `planned/` with that decided.
